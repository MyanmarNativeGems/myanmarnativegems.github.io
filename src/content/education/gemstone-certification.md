A gemological laboratory report, often called a certificate, is an independent expert opinion on a specific stone. For significant purchases it is the single most useful document a buyer can ask for.

## What a report covers

A typical colored stone report identifies the material, records measurements and weight, and states whether treatments were detected. Some laboratories also offer an opinion on geographic origin for certain stones, based on the stone's internal characteristics and trace chemistry. Origin opinions are expert judgments rather than absolute facts, and reputable laboratories present them as such.

## What a report does not cover

Most laboratory reports do not appraise monetary value, and a report is not a guarantee of beauty. Two stones with similar reports can look meaningfully different in person. The report answers what the stone is; your own eyes answer whether you love it.

## Reading a report

Check that the report's measurements and weight match the stone offered, that the report comes from a recognized laboratory, and that any treatment findings are stated clearly. If a listing mentions a report, ask to see the document itself.

## Our practice

Where a stone in our collection has a laboratory report, the listing links to it. Stones without a linked report have not been certified by a laboratory at the time of listing, and we are open about that. If you would like a stone certified before purchase, ask us and we will discuss the options.
