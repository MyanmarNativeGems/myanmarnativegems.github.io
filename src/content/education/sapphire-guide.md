Sapphire is gem-quality corundum in any color other than red. Blue is the classic and best-known color, produced by traces of iron and titanium, but sapphire also occurs in pink, yellow, green, violet, and the rare orange-pink known as padparadscha.

## Color

In blue sapphire, the most valued stones show a velvety, well-saturated blue with good evenness across the stone. Very dark or grayish stones trade lower. In fancy colors, purity and strength of the hue drive value. As with ruby, judging color in more than one light source is worth the effort.

## Durability

Like ruby, sapphire measures 9 on the Mohs hardness scale, which makes it one of the most durable colored gemstones and well suited to frequent wear.

## Clarity and cut

Sapphires are generally cleaner than rubies, so eye-visible inclusions carry more weight in valuation. A well-executed cut keeps color even and avoids windowing, where the center of the stone looks washed out.

## Treatments

Heat treatment is standard practice in the sapphire trade and accepted when disclosed. Diffusion treatments, which add color from the surface inward, are a different matter and should always be identified. Ask how any specific stone has been treated.
