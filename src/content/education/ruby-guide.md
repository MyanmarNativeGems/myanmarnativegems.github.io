Ruby is the red variety of the mineral corundum. Trace amounts of chromium give the stone its color and, in many stones, a soft internal glow under daylight. All other colors of gem-quality corundum are called sapphire.

## Color

Color is the most important quality factor in ruby. The most sought-after stones show a vivid, well-saturated red that is neither too dark nor too pink. Because color perception depends on lighting, a ruby should ideally be viewed in more than one light source before a decision is made.

## Durability

Corundum measures 9 on the Mohs hardness scale, second only to diamond among classic gem materials. This makes ruby a practical choice for jewelry that is worn often, once it is eventually set.

## Clarity and cut

Most rubies contain natural inclusions, and minor inclusions are generally accepted in a way they are not for diamonds. Fine silk-like inclusions can even soften the color attractively. Cut quality affects how evenly the color reads across the stone, so symmetry and light return matter more than a nominally ideal shape.

## Treatments

Heat treatment is common in the ruby trade and is widely accepted when disclosed. Other treatments, such as glass filling, affect value and care requirements significantly. Always ask how a specific stone has been treated, and expect a clear answer.

## Before you buy

Ask about the stone's weight, treatment, and any laboratory documentation. For any stone in our collection, we are glad to share what is known about it and to provide additional photographs on request.
