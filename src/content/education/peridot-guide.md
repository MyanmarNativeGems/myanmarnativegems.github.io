Peridot is the gem variety of olivine, a common rock-forming mineral, though clean material large enough to cut is far less common than the mineral itself. Unlike most colored gems, its green comes from iron within its basic chemistry rather than a trace impurity, which is why peridot occurs in only one color family.

## Color

Peridot ranges from a yellowish green to a deep olive green. The most prized stones show a pure, well-saturated green with minimal brown or yellow undertone.

## Durability

Peridot measures 6.5 to 7 on the Mohs hardness scale, softer than ruby, sapphire, or spinel. It suits pendants and earrings especially well; rings warrant a little more care, and ultrasonic cleaning is best avoided.

## Clarity and cut

Peridot often contains small dark inclusions (chromite crystals), sometimes surrounded by a faint disc-shaped halo, which is characteristic of the material rather than a flaw. Eye-clean stones carry a premium. Good cutting keeps the color even across the stone.

## Treatments

Peridot is rarely treated in the trade. Its color occurs exactly as it forms, with no history of heat or other enhancement.

## Before you buy

Ask about weight, color tone, and clarity. Because peridot is a single-color gem, judging saturation and steering clear of overly brownish or pale stones is most of the work.
