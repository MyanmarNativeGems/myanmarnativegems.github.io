Most colored gemstones on the market today have been treated in some way to improve their appearance. Treatment is not inherently a problem; undisclosed treatment is. Understanding the common treatments helps you ask the right questions.

## Heat treatment

Heating is the oldest and most widely accepted treatment, used extensively on ruby and sapphire to improve color and clarity. The results are generally stable and permanent. Heated stones trade at lower prices than comparable unheated stones, which is exactly why disclosure matters.

## Filling

Fractures reaching the surface of a stone can be filled with glass or resin to improve apparent clarity. Glass-filled rubies in particular require careful handling, as the filler can be damaged by heat, acids, and even some cleaning solutions. Filled stones are valued far below untreated or simply heated material.

## Dyeing and impregnation

Porous gem materials can be bleached, dyed, or impregnated with polymer to improve their apparent color and stability. These treatments change value substantially and are not always visible to the eye, which is why laboratory testing is the reliable way to identify them.

## Diffusion and irradiation

Diffusion introduces coloring elements into the surface of a stone at high temperature. Irradiation is used on some materials to alter color. Both must be disclosed, and both affect value.
