Jade refers to two distinct minerals: jadeite and nephrite. Jadeite is the rarer and generally more valuable of the two, and Myanmar's deposits have historically been the world's principal source of fine jadeite. Nephrite has its own long history, particularly in Chinese carving traditions.

## Color and translucency

The most prized jadeite shows an even, vivid green combined with high translucency, a combination often described in the trade as imperial jade. Fine jade also occurs in lavender, white, yellow, and near-black. Unlike faceted stones, jade is usually judged as much by its texture and glow as by its color alone.

## Cut and form

Jade is typically fashioned as cabochons, beads, bangles, and carvings rather than faceted stones. In these forms, evenness of color, fineness of texture, and freedom from visible fractures are the key quality markers.

## Treatments

The jade trade distinguishes untreated jadeite from jadeite that has been bleached and impregnated with polymer, or dyed. These treatments change value substantially and are not always visible to the eye, which is why laboratory testing plays a larger role in jade than in most gems. Always ask whether a piece has been tested.

## Before you buy

For jade, documentation matters more than for almost any other gem material. Ask about testing, treatment, and provenance of the specific piece. We are glad to share whatever is documented for any jade in our collection.
