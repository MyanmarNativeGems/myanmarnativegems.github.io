Tourmaline is a complex borosilicate mineral, and its chemistry allows an unusually wide range of trace elements into the crystal. That is why one mineral family produces such a broad spread of color, and why a single crystal can even show two or three colors at once.

## Color

Tourmaline occurs in nearly every hue: pink and red (rubellite), green (verdelite), blue-green (indicolite), and bi-color or "watermelon" stones that combine several colors in one piece. Depth and evenness of color are the main drivers of value.

## Durability

Tourmaline measures 7 to 7.5 on the Mohs hardness scale, comfortably durable for rings and everyday wear.

## Clarity and cut

Tourmaline typically grows with fine, thread-like internal cavities running along its length; very clean stones exist but are less common in the richest colors. Cutting is usually oriented to show the deepest color face-up, since color can shift depending on viewing direction.

## Treatments

Heat and irradiation are used elsewhere in the trade to improve some tourmaline colors. Every stone we sell is natural and untreated, so the color is exactly as mined.

## Before you buy

Ask about color, weight, and clarity, and note the shape: pear, cushion, and elongated cuts are all common in tourmaline. We're glad to discuss any stone's particular color in more depth.
