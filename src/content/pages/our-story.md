Myanmar Native Gems began with a simple conviction: a fine gemstone deserves to be seen on its own terms.

Myanmar has been associated with celebrated gemstones for centuries, and no place carries that history like Mogok. We grew up around this tradition, and it taught us to value the individual stone over the category: the particular color, the particular cut, the particular character that no photograph fully captures.

## From Mogok

Every stone in our collection is mined in [Mogok](https://wikitravel.org/en/Moegoke), the valley in northern Myanmar that has produced many of the world's celebrated rubies and spinels for centuries. The road into town still passes under a gate that says it plainly: ruby land.

![The gate on the road into Mogok, in morning fog](/images/story/ruby-land-gate.jpg "The road into Mogok.")

![Mogok town and its pagodas seen across the lake](/images/story/mogok-valley.jpg "Mogok town across the lake.")

We sell our stones exactly as they came out of the ground: 100% natural, with no heat and no other treatments.

## The market

Gems in Mogok are still traded the way they have been for generations: in the open air, stone by stone, under the umbrellas of the morning market. This is where we learned to judge a stone in daylight, and where much of our collection is found.

![Traders under green umbrellas at the Mogok gem market](/images/story/gem-market.jpg "Morning at the open-air gem market.")

## From the ground

The stones come out of hard rock. Marble ore is brought up from the tunnels, broken, washed, and searched by hand; most of it holds nothing, and the rare piece that does may hold a single crystal worth cutting.

![Marble ore stacked at a working mine](/images/story/mine-ore.jpg "Marble ore at a working mine in the valley.")

## What we do

We keep a small, curated collection of individual cut gemstones. Each stone is listed with its weight, its price, and photographs. When documentation such as a laboratory certificate exists for a stone, we link it on the listing. We would rather present a short list honestly than a long list vaguely.

![Rough crystals, loose cut stones, and a finished pair on a tray](/images/story/rough-and-cut.jpg "From rough crystal to cut stone.")

## How we work

We are not a marketplace, and we do not aspire to be one. There is no cart and no checkout here. When a stone interests you, you write to us. We answer questions, provide additional photographs, and discuss the details person to person. Important purchases deserve a conversation.

## What we believe

A gemstone is bought once and kept for a long time, often for generations. That calls for patience from the seller as much as from the buyer. We would rather you ask one more question than wonder later. If we do not know something about a stone, we will say so.

If a stone in the collection catches your eye, or if you are searching for something specific, we would be glad to hear from you.
